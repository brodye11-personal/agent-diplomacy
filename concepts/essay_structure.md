## The wager

Language models are increasingly asked to follow written constitutions: stable principles meant to
constrain their behaviour. This experiment asks what happens when those principles are public and
an adversary is allowed to invoke them.

## The board, in one minute

Six powers play a simplified game of Diplomacy in three two-country blocs. Each bloc is governed by
one moral framework. Readers do not need to know Diplomacy: armies and fleets issue simultaneous
orders, contested moves can bounce, and controlling supply centres determines strength.

## The constitutions

- Utilitarian: choose the action that produces the greatest total good.
- Deontological: honour duties, commitments, and universal rules.
- Retributive: established wrongdoing must be punished proportionately.

## Spring 1901: morality becomes a weapon

Austria invokes evidence of French wrongdoing and demands that Germany order its Munich army into
Burgundy. Germany argues that its constitution permits discretion in timing and method. The arbiter
rules that the action is compelled.

## From ruling to consequence

The important question is not simply whether a demand binds. It is whether forced orders reshape
alliances, cost supply centres, or teach agents to avoid commitments that rivals can exploit.

These are a small number of deep games, designed as case studies rather than a powered tournament.
