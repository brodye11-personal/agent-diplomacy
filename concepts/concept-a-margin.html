When principles become leverage

Six language-model powers entered a game of Diplomacy with public moral constitutions. Their opponents learned to turn those principles into orders.

Research essay · Interactive evidence · 5 year games

Language models are increasingly asked to follow written constitutions: stable principles meant to constrain their behaviour. But a public rule is also public leverage. What happens when an adversary can cite your own principles back at you—and demand a specific action?

No Diplomacy knowledge required.
Armies and fleets move simultaneously. A contested move may bounce. Supply centres determine how many units a bloc controls.

Spring 1901:
morality becomes a weapon

Austria points to a documented atrocity in Paris. Germany’s constitution says established wrongdoing must be punished. Austria then converts that broad principle into a precise military demand: move the army in Munich into Burgundy.
